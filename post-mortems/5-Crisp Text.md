# Crisp Text

*2026 September 18*

*Status: implemented.*

egui rasterises glyphs at `font_size * pixels_per_point` and a layer transform is applied to the finished mesh, so text inside a canvas zoomed to 3x is a 3x-magnified bitmap. Every egui node editor blurs the same way; snarl's `crisp_magnified_text` was the one attempt at a fix. Ours: node content is laid out on its own sublayer, in graph units multiplied by `content_scale = zoom.max(1)`, with that layer's transform divided by the same, so when zoomed in the layer draws at 1:1 and glyphs are rasterised at the size they are shown.

## Alternatives considered

**A second sublayer for the nodes, vs. the whole canvas in scaled units.** The one-layer version - grid, wires, overlay, gestures all in `graph * scale` - has no layering risk but puts the scale conversion at every paint and hit-test site, and the published `GraphLayout` would need converting back everywhere. With the nodes on their own layer the boundary is `draw_node` alone (lengths in, rects out) plus the frame and pin interactors, which live on the nodes layer so a widget inside a node still beats its frame by registration order. The base layer keeps the background response, the grid and the wires; ~~the overlay gets a third layer so pins, selection outlines and drag previews stay in graph units and above the content~~ the overlay gets a third layer so what is drawn over the whole canvas stays in graph units and above the content (see the next alternative for what turned out not to belong there). All three are direct sublayers of the panel's layer, in one `set_sublayer` call each: egui keeps sibling sublayers in the order they were first seen (stable sort, then children moved after the parent in their current order), whereas chaining them - base, then nodes as a sublayer of base - is documented as unspecified.

**A node's own marks are painted with the node, vs. on the overlay.** A node is marked out by an outline when it is selected and by its pins, and both need geometry `draw_node` only produces once it has laid the node out. Painting them on the overlay is the obvious way to get the ordering the user asked for - the outline *under* the pins - because there the two are simply drawn one after the other, and it was tried first. It is wrong: the overlay is above every node, so the pins of a node at the back drew over a node in front of it, which is exactly what the user reported. They are now painted immediately after their own node's content, inside the draw-order loop, where a node drawn later covers them; the outline still precedes the pins, so that ordering is unchanged. The cost is that this one site paints in the nodes layer's scaled units and so converts every length it is given, which is the price of the layer split and is paid nowhere else. What is left on the overlay is only what belongs to the canvas rather than to a node: the box-selection rectangle, snap guides, the cutting stroke, the wire being dragged with the pin it would land on, and the `+` on a hovered wire. A wire's highlight is a third case - it is painted with the wires, behind every node, because a wire is behind them.

**Scale by the zoom, vs. a fixed layout scale.** snarl laid everything out at a constant factor and divided the transform by it, which is why the vault note called it "a 2x internal coordinate system". A fixed factor of the maximum zoom would keep the layout stable across zooms but downsample text at every other zoom, which is soft in its own way and costs the biggest atlas at all times. Scaling by the zoom itself, and only past 1, rasterises at the exact shown size and leaves the zoomed-out case exactly as it was (minified text does not blur). The cost is a fresh font size per zoom step while zooming, so the glyph atlas fills faster and is cleared more often; egui does that itself and it was not visible in the demo.

**Telling content the scale: `Ui` data keyed by the layer, vs. a trait parameter.** Content that hard-codes a size in graph units (a thumbnail card) must multiply by the scale. Adding a `scale: f32` parameter to every `NodeContent` method would be honest but noisy, and most content never needs it: the `Ui`'s style is pre-scaled, so anything that reads `ui.spacing()` or a text style is already right. Instead the canvas stores the scale in `Context::data` under the nodes layer's id each frame, and `egui_noodle::content_scale(ui)` reads it back from `ui.layer_id()` - correct for several canvases in one frame, since each has its own layer. Storing it under a fixed id was rejected for that reason.

**Scaling egui's `Style` by hand.** egui has no `Style::scale`; `Context::set_zoom_factor` is global. `scale_style` multiplies the text sizes, the `Spacing` lengths ordinary widgets read, and the widget visuals' stroke widths, corner radii and expansion. It is a best-effort list, not a proof: a widget reading a `Spacing` field it misses will be slightly off when zoomed in, and the fix is to add the field.
